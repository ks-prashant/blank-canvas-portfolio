# Positioning & Narrative

A narrative framing of the career arc, built from the facts in `03-projects/` and `02-career-timeline.md`.

## Core identity

Prashant Singh positions himself as an **AI Product Manager and Builder** — a product manager who leads with strategy and business judgment, and who also builds the AI systems himself rather than only writing specs for an engineering team to implement. The judgment is the primary claim; the hands-on building is the proof that makes the judgment credible, evidenced by two solo-built, live production AI products (Automjet Sales Agent, Grounded Governance) alongside his employer-side product work.

## The arc, as a story

Three phases, each building the next: **analytics and hybrid analyst/PM work** (Infosys — regulated banking, Prashaste, Ola — mobility, including his one classical-ML project, fare prediction) → **payments and monetization product ownership** in a regulated, transactional marketplace (nurture.farm — fintech) → **generative and agentic AI product ownership** in a B2B SaaS platform (TopHire — HR tech), plus two solo AI builds outside employment (Automjet Sales Agent, Grounded Governance) that demonstrate the same judgment applied hands-on, without an employer's engineering team.

The regulated-payments background is a genuine differentiator for AI-product roles that touch money, compliance, or high-stakes automation — most AI PMs haven't shipped an RBI-compliant, NBFC-partnered lending product. See `03-projects/professional-experience/nurture-farm/lending.md`.

## What characterizes his work

Four things, consistently evidenced across every project in this book:

1. **Judgment** — deciding what's worth building, and at what scope. Evidenced by outcomes tied to specific decisions he directly owned: 2x growth in Monthly Transacting Users at nurture.farm (his north-star metric there, 4k→8k), +140% recruiter productivity at TopHire — both traceable to specific product mechanisms, not vague strategic narrative. He's careful to distinguish this from company-wide numbers like nurture.farm's 5x GMV growth over the same period, which his product work contributed to but doesn't claim sole credit for.
2. **Shipping, hands-on** — he builds the AI himself. Evidenced by two live products: Automjet Sales Agent (in production at a real dealership) and Grounded Governance (public URL anyone can use), both built solo, end to end.
3. **Rigor** — measured vendor bake-offs before committing to a model or tool, A/B testing before full rollout, deterministic output over inferred guesses, and honest accuracy figures stated with their caveats attached. This is what separates an AI product manager from a product manager who has simply used AI tools.
4. **Range** — nine-plus years across fintech, HR tech, marketplaces, mobility, and regulated banking, not one industry revisited under different titles.

His title has been **Product Manager** for past few years, not "Senior Product Manager" — roughly 9.5 years of total experience, of which about 5 years were specifically in a Product Management role (nurture.farm, TopHire); the earlier years were analyst and consulting roles. He is currently between roles, open to new product and AI-builder opportunities — his most recent role, at TopHire, ended in May 2026. The "top 1% among 600+ trainees" achievement is specific to a training program at Infosys at the start of his career (see `03-projects/professional-experience/infosys/experience.md`), not a general claim about his standing today. The business's overall GMV also grew 5x ($15M→$80M) over the same period — a real, resume-sourced number, but a company-wide one that sales and other teams also drove. He states MTU as his outcome and GMV as business context his work contributed to, not a number he claims to have single-handedly produced. See `03-projects/professional-experience/nurture-farm/company-context.md` for the roadmap-level outcome, and `checkout-optimization.md`, `cashback-builder.md`, and `lending.md` for the individual mechanisms behind the MTU growth.
