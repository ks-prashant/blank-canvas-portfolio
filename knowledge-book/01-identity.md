### Why he left TopHire

**Limited growth, driven by a shrinking team.** When he joined, TopHire's product org was 2 product managers and 7–8 engineers. Over his three years there, it contracted rather than expanded — by the end of his tenure he was the **solo product manager** for the product, covering both the B2B and B2C sides, working with **3–4 engineers**.

His reasoning is structural, not a complaint about the work: a product org that is shrinking rather than growing caps what a PM in it can take on. Scope can't expand, there's no one to grow into managing, and the ceiling arrives early. He wanted to keep growing; the org couldn't offer the room. This is worth stating plainly — the work itself went well (the four shipped AI products, +140% productivity, ~25% time-to-hire reduction all came out of this period, much of it *while* the team was contracting), and leaving was about trajectory rather than dissatisfaction with the product or the outcomes.

Note that the shipped results are more impressive read against this backdrop, not less: the AI product suite was delivered by a shrinking team, with him as the only PM on it by the end.

### Why he left without another offer already in hand

The honest answer, and the natural follow-up question to the above: **he couldn't run a serious interview process alongside the job.** As the solo PM on a contracting team, there wasn't room to do both properly. So he made it a deliberate sequence rather than trying to overlap them — **leave, take a break to learn, then interview.** The break is being used for exactly that: building and learning hands-on (see the solo builds in `02-career-timeline.md` — Grounded Governance in particular is a full retrieval-augmented product with its own evaluation harness, built solo during this period; `03-projects/personal-projects/grounded-governance/grounded-governance.md`).

This is a considered decision, not a gap to explain away — he chose to give the search his full attention rather than run it badly in the margins of a demanding role.
